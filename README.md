## Class Reviews

* **Overall:** This is undeniably one of the core fundamental courses that every Computer Science student must master. While it might not seem as heavily emphasized for Cybersecurity or AI/DS majors compared to pure Software Engineering students (which explains why this specific course variant is only 2 credits compared to the 3-credit version taken by other programs), building a solid DSA foundation is still crucial.
* **Course Description:** The grading system follows a 40/60 scheme. Specifically, 40% of your overall grade evaluates your in-class progress throughout the semester (including your midterm score and bonus points for diligence/participation), while the remaining 60% is determined by the final exam. The course spans approximately 15 weeks, focusing on both theoretical lectures and practical problem-solving. You have the flexibility to use either Python or C++ for coding assignments, depending on your personal preference.
* **Instructor:** Mrs. Phuong is highly meticulous and exceptionally clear when explaining complex concepts and principles, making her lessons very easy to follow. While she is quite easygoing and doesn't strictly take attendance, she highly values a serious learning attitude. Therefore, active and respectful participation is expected. Actively contributing to the lecture with ideas and answering questions is an excellent way to earn bonus points toward your in-class progress score.
* **Midterm Exam:** The scope of the midterm is limited to the first three sections of the course: Algorithm Complexity (Ch. 1), Basic Algorithms (Ch. 2), and Basic Data Structures (Ch. 3). Open-book materials are allowed (typically, students print and bring the slides for the first 3 chapters). The exam consists of two parts:
  * **Quiz:** Contains about 23 multiple-choice questions. Be careful, as some questions are multiple-response (having more than one correct answer).
  * **Written Test:** Consists of 2 essay-style questions requiring you to design an algorithm (you can intepret the ideas through using pseudocode or algorithm procedure) and demonstrate your comprehension of a given specific algorithm application and its result.
* **Final Exam:** The final exam consists of two parts, each accounting for 50% of the total score:
  * **Part 1 (Theory Quiz):** A multiple-choice quiz of about 28 questions. This part focuses heavily on theoretical concepts regarding Data Structures such as Stacks, Queues, Max Heaps, Binary Trees, Red-Black Trees, and Binary Search Trees.
  * **Part 2 (Coding Test):** A practical coding test featuring three problems, all scaling around the Medium difficulty level, worth 3, 3, and 4 points respectively. 
    * The first problem is often a "giveaway" question that can be easily solved using a simple brute-force approach. 
    * The second is typically a standard algorithmic problem. 
    * The third is a fill-in-the-blank coding question where you are provided with a sequence of operations on a specific Data Structure and must complete the corresponding code blocks. 
    * *Note:* During my semester, the algorithmic scope for this coding part was strictly limited to Backtracking (from Chapter 2) and did not include advanced data structures like Red-Black Trees or Graph algorithms.
  * We were allowed to bring a single A4 cheat sheet into the exam room. I highly recommend using this sheet to meticulously note down the core mathematical principles of the algorithms to assist you with the quiz portion.
  * Choosing the right code editor for the coding section (Part 2) is a crucial factor you must consider. For Python users, some lab computers have Thonny installed, which is arguably the best and most stable choice for this exam. However, if it's unavailable, you will have to rely on Jupyter Notebook. Make sure you did practice with Jupyter beforehand, as its interface can be quite tricky to navigate under time pressure if you are not used to it. For C++ users, although I didn't use C++ for my exam, you can safely rely on Code::Blocks, as it is pre-installed on every computer in the exam room.
